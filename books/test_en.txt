The sun is up and the sky is blue. It is a warm day in June. A boy and his dog go out to play. The dog is small and brown. It has big ears and a wet nose. The boy has a red cap on his head. He is tall and thin. He has a big grin on his face.

They run to the park. The park is not far from home. It has green grass and tall trees. A pond is in the park. Ducks swim on the pond. The boy sits on a wood bench. His dog sits by his feet. The dog wags its tail. It is glad to be with the boy.

A bird sings in a tree. The sun is hot now. The boy feels warm. He takes off his red cap. The dog laps up cool pond water. Soon more kids come to the park. They fly kites and play ball. The boy and his dog join them. They all have a lot of fun.

At noon, the boy and the dog go home. They are both tired but glad. They had a good day in the sun. The boy gives the dog some food and a bowl of fresh water. Then they both nap on the soft rug.


A man and a cat live in a small flat in the city. The cat is old and gray. Its name is Mist. Mist likes to sit by the big glass door. The sun shines in and Mist naps there all day long. The man, whose name is Sam, works from home. He taps on his lap top at a desk near the door. Mist likes to be near Sam as he works.

Sam goes out once a day to get food. He walks to a shop at the end of the road. He buys milk, eggs, bread, and fish for Mist. Mist is glad to see Sam come back. The cat rubs its head on Sam's leg and purrs. Sam pats the cat and says kind words softly.

At dusk, Sam and Mist sit on the old red sofa. Sam reads a book. Mist curls up in his lap. The room is dim and calm. A lamp glows by the sofa. It is a nice end to the day. Sam and Mist are pals and they like their life as it is. No rush, no fuss, just peace.


A girl and her mom go to a fair. The fair has rides and games. A big wheel turns high up in the air. The girl wants to ride it. She and her mom get in a seat. The wheel goes up slow. At the top they see the whole town. The cars and homes look so tiny. The girl grins and waves her arms in joy.

After the ride, they eat pink sweet floss. It melts on the tongue. Then they try a game. The girl has to toss a ring on a peg. She tries four times and on the last toss she wins! She gets a plush bear as a prize. The bear is soft and white with a blue bow.

The sun goes down. The lights at the fair glow red and gold. The girl is so glad. It is a day she will not soon for get. Her mom holds her hand as they walk to the car. The girl hugs her new toy bear tight. She is fast a sleep by the time they get home.


Deep in the woods, a fox and a hare talk by a log. The fox has red fur and a bushy tail. The hare has long ears and a white pelt. "I saw a fat hen by the barn," says the fox. "Let's go and get it!" The hare hops back. "No, I do not eat hens. I eat grass and bark and buds." The fox sighs. "You do not know what you miss."

A wise old owl in a hoot tree hears them. "Hush," says the owl. "Each of us has our own diet. The hare is right to eat what suits its kind." The fox nods slow. "I see your point. I will go find my own food." And the fox slinks off in the dusk.

The hare and the owl sit in the calm of the wood. The stars come out one by one. "It is a fine night," says the hare. "Yes, a fine night," says the owl. Soon all is still and the moon glows pale on the tree tops.


A boy named Leo helps his dad in a small yard by their home. The yard has two apple trees and a row of red flowers. Leo has a green pail and a short rake. His dad has a long hose and a wide hat. They work side by side in the cool of the morn.

Leo pulls small weeds from the soft dirt. He puts them in the pail. His dad sprays water on the roots of the trees. Drops of water shine like glass in the sun. A bee hums by the flowers. A snail moves slow near a flat stone.

When the work is done, they sit on the back step to rest. Dad cuts one ripe apple in half. Leo takes a big bite and smiles. "Sweet," he says with a grin. Dad laughs and nods. They feel proud of their neat and bright yard.


In a town by the sea, a girl named May wakes up at dawn. She and her aunt run a small bread shop. The shop is warm and full of good smells. May ties on a clean apron and sets cups on a shelf. Her aunt kneads dough on a big wood table.

Soon, the first loaf comes out of the hot oven. Its crust is brown and crisp. May puts the loaf in the front window. A man comes in to buy buns. Then two kids come in for jam rolls. The bell on the door rings all morn long.

At noon, May and her aunt share soup and fresh bread in the back room. They hear gulls cry near the dock. "It is a busy day," says May. "Yes," says her aunt, "but a good one." They go back to work with glad hearts and warm hands.


On a rainy day, Tom and his sis stay in the house. Rain taps on the roof and runs down the glass. Tom finds a box of old board games in the hall. His sis, Eve, picks one with bright cards and small game men. They sit at the low table in the den.

At first, Tom wins two rounds in a row. Eve folds her arms and gives him a mock frown. Then she gets a lucky card and jumps far ahead. Tom laughs and says, "No fair!" Eve laughs too and taps the table with joy. Their mom brings warm milk and toast.

By late day, the rain stops. A thin beam of sun breaks through the gray sky. Tom and Eve look out the door at a wide wet path. "Let's go for a short walk," says Mom. They put on boots and step out, still talking of the game.


At the edge of a lake, a class goes on a day trip with their teacher, Ms. Lin. Each child has a note pad and a pen. Ms. Lin asks them to watch the sky, the trees, and the waves. "Write what you see and hear," she says in a soft voice.

Nia sees a line of ants near a log and writes it down. Ben hears frogs croak in the reeds and smiles. Two ducks flap up from the shore and skim low over the water. The class sits still for ten full minutes, just to listen. They hear wind, wings, and small splashes.

When it is time to leave, Ms. Lin asks each child to read one line from the page. The lines are short but full of life. "The lake is like a gray mirror," says one child. "The grass bows when the wind runs by," says another. Ms. Lin claps and says they all did fine work.
